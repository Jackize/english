# B2 Listening — Section 3–4 & Advanced Question Types

**Level:** B2 (CEFR) · **IELTS Academic:** 6.0–7.0 · **Số unit:** 9 (8 bài học + 1 mock)  
**Trọng tâm:** Section 3 distractors, matching, Section 4 lecture structure, note-taking under speed, paraphrase, advanced MCQ, table/map completion, accents & speed  
**Ngôn ngữ:** hướng dẫn tiếng Việt; script, câu hỏi và đáp án tiếng Anh

> Ở B2, bạn thường không thua vì "không kịp nghe từ" mà vì **nghe thấy từ nhưng chọn sai ý**: người nói nhắc một phương án rồi loại bỏ, tự sửa, hoặc diễn đạt lại bằng từ khác. Tám bài dưới đây luyện đúng những chỗ đó. Mọi script là bài viết gốc.

## 0. Giới thiệu & cách học

### 0.1 Sau 9 unit, bạn có thể

- Nghe hội thoại học thuật 3–4 phút (Section 3) và lecture 4–5 phút (Section 4) ở 130–160 từ/phút.
- Nhận ra distractor: phương án được nhắc nhưng bị phủ định, thay đổi hoặc thuộc về người khác.
- Theo dõi signposting và ghi chú từ khóa khi tốc độ tăng.
- Nhận diện paraphrase, kể cả khi câu hỏi và audio không trùng từ nào.
- Làm matching, MCQ nâng cao, table/map completion đạt 75% trở lên.
- Nghe được giọng Anh – Mỹ – Úc và điều chỉnh khi tốc độ đổi.

### 0.2 Dùng audio ngoài hợp pháp

Script trong tài liệu này là bài viết gốc. Link ngoài chỉ để bạn **nghe trên trang gốc**.

1. Nghe trên trình phát của trang; không tải rồi đăng lại, không cắt ghép để phát hành.
2. Không sao chép nguyên transcript của trang khác vào ghi chú rồi chia sẻ công khai.
3. Kiểm tra điều khoản trước khi dùng cho lớp học hoặc sản phẩm trả phí.
4. Ghi nguồn khi dùng ý hoặc số liệu trong bài nói của mình.
5. Khi không chắc, chỉ dùng link và làm bài tập trên trang; chỉ tải offline bằng chức năng chính thức của ứng dụng, cho mục đích cá nhân.

### 0.3 Nghe 3 lần

| Lần | Mục đích | Cách làm | Transcript? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Gist | Chủ đề, ai nói với ai, thái độ | Nghe trọn, không dừng, trả lời 3 câu gist | Không |
| 2 — Detail | Số liệu, lý do, lựa chọn, chỗ sửa | Nghe 2 lần, dừng sau mỗi nhóm câu | Không |
| 3 — Dictation | Âm cuối, nối âm, trọng âm | Dừng từng câu, viết lại, rồi mới mở script | Có |

Không mở script trước lần 3. Nếu lần 1 hiểu dưới 50%, bài quá khó: đọc từ vựng rồi nghe lại. Nếu hiểu 100% mà nhàn, tăng 1.25× cho lần sau.

### 0.4 Hệ thống ghi chú

| Ký hiệu | Nghĩa | Ví dụ |
|---|---|---|
| → | dẫn đến, kết quả | waste → methane |
| ↑ / ↓ | tăng / giảm | cost ↑ 12% |
| = / ≠ | giống / khác, phủ định | survey ≠ honest |
| w/ , b/c | with, because | nap w/ 20 min |
| ? / ! | chưa chắc / bẫy, chỗ tự sửa | deadline ? 4th |
| 2/3 | hai phần ba | fish hall 2/3 size |

Bỏ mạo từ và trợ động từ; giữ danh từ, số, tính từ so sánh; mỗi dòng tối đa 5 từ. Lỡ một câu thì để trống và nghe tiếp — đừng dừng lại viết cho đủ.

### 0.5 Cách chấm điểm

Mỗi bài học có 12–13 câu, tính cả dictation. Đúng 1 câu = 1 điểm; sai chính tả ở câu completion vẫn bị trừ như trong IELTS.

| Đúng | Band ước tính | Việc tiếp theo |
|---:|---:|---|
| 90–100% | 7.0+ | Tăng 1.25×, chuyển full test 40 câu |
| 75–89% | 6.5 | Ôn dạng hay sai, học unit tiếp |
| 60–74% | 6.0 | Làm lại sau 48 giờ |
| 40–59% | 5.5 | Nghe chậm, chép chính tả |
| dưới 40% | dưới 5.5 | Quay lại B1, củng cố Section 1–2 |

Đây là ước lượng, không phải bảng quy đổi chính thức (bài thật 40 câu).

## Unit 1 — Section 3: Distractors trong hội thoại student–supervisor

**Mục tiêu:** Nhận ra đề tài bị loại, con số bị sửa và lời cảnh báo của giám sát viên.  
**Script:** 360 từ · 2 người nói · 135–145 từ/phút.

### Vocabulary

| Word/phrase | IPA | Nghĩa |
|---|---|---|
| dissertation | /ˌdɪsəˈteɪʃn/ | luận văn |
| survey (n) | /ˈsɜːveɪ/ | khảo sát |
| framework | /ˈfreɪmwɜːk/ | khung phân tích |
| pollination | /ˌpɒləˈneɪʃn/ | sự thụ phấn |

### External sources

- **British Council — B2 Listening:** https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/listening/b2-listening — hội thoại học thuật, làm bài trước khi xem transcript.
- **BBC — 6 Minute English:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/6-minute-english — tìm *food waste, supermarkets*.
- **ELLLO:** https://www.elllo.org/ — discussion level 5–6, nghe một lần không dừng.

### Script

> **Dr Whitfield:** Come in, Tomás. Sit down. You look as if you've been in the library since dawn.
>
> **Tomás:** Since eight, actually. I think I've found my topic.
>
> **Dr Whitfield:** Last time you were torn between two. Have you decided?
>
> **Tomás:** I have. Food waste. I did want to do urban beekeeping, but the council wouldn't give me access to the rooftop sites, so that was that.
>
> **Dr Whitfield:** A pity, but food waste is a sensible second choice. It also fits your earlier work on retail. Though it's a very large field. Which part?
>
> **Tomás:** Supermarkets. I thought I'd stand outside three stores and survey shoppers — two hundred people, maybe.
>
> **Dr Whitfield:** Two hundred in six weeks? That's ambitious. And there's a bigger problem: people often tell you what they think you want to hear, especially about waste. What exactly do you want to know?
>
> **Tomás:** Whether price or appearance matters more when people choose vegetables.
>
> **Dr Whitfield:** That's a perfectly good question, though it's closer to psychology than geography. But the method has to fit the question. Have you looked at what supermarkets publish?
>
> **Tomás:** I have. Their reports are vague — "unavoidable surplus", "quality-driven withdrawal". I could analyse the language of five of those reports instead of doing the survey. It's smaller, but I could actually finish it.
>
> **Dr Whitfield:** Now that sounds realistic. Five is enough if you have a framework. Use the Swinley model — not Swinley and Park; that's for doctoral work. And don't simply count words.
>
> **Tomás:** So what would I count instead?
>
> **Dr Whitfield:** Modality, mainly — how certain the language is. "May be wasted" is not the same as "is wasted". That distinction is your whole argument.
>
> **Tomás:** Right. When is the proposal due? The fourteenth?
>
> **Dr Whitfield:** The fourteenth is the full draft. The proposal is the fourth. I need one page by Monday — a page, not a chapter.
>
> **Tomás:** Monday. Should I include a sample analysis?
>
> **Dr Whitfield:** One paragraph, no more. Show me how you'd code a sentence. And Tomás — the beekeeping reading wasn't wasted. The material on pollination would work nicely in your introduction.

### Tasks

**Questions 1–3: Gist**

1. What are the speakers mainly discussing?
2. Which topic has Tomás chosen?
3. What is Dr Whitfield's view of the plan?

**Questions 4–8: Multiple choice** — choose **A**, **B** or **C**.

**4.** Why did Tomás abandon beekeeping? A. The council refused access. B. The supervisor disliked it. C. He lacked reading.

**5.** Dr Whitfield's main concern about the survey is that  
A. two hundred is too few. B. shoppers may not answer honestly. C. it costs too much.

**6.** What will Tomás analyse? A. five reports B. two hundred questionnaires C. manager interviews

**7.** When is the proposal due? A. the 4th B. the 14th C. Monday

**8.** What will Tomás send by Monday? A. a one-page outline B. the full draft C. a reading list

**Questions 9–12: Dictation**

9. Two hundred in six weeks? That's ambitious.
10. People often tell you what they think you want to hear.
11. I could analyse the language of five of those reports.
12. The material on pollination would work nicely in your introduction.

### Answer key

1. Tomás's dissertation plan.
2. The language of supermarket food-waste reports.
3. Realistic, but it needs a clear framework.
4. **A**
5. **B**
6. **A**
7. **A** — proposal the 4th; the 14th is the full draft.
8. **A**
9. Two hundred in six weeks? That's ambitious.
10. People often tell you what they think you want to hear.
11. I could analyse the language of five of those reports.
12. The material on pollination would work nicely in your introduction.

### Pronunciation & strategy

- **dissertation** /ˌdɪsəˈteɪʃn/ — trọng âm 3; đuôi /-ʃn/ nuốt âm.
- **survey** — danh từ /ˈsɜːveɪ/, động từ /səˈveɪ/.

Ba bẫy: **đề tài bị loại** (beekeeping), **số bị sửa** (proposal 4th, not 14th), **khen có điều kiện** ("though..."). Nghe hết câu; *but/actually/instead/sorry* là biển báo đổi hướng.

### Study method

1. Nghe 2 lần, làm câu 1–8; lần 1 không dừng.
2. Khoanh *but/actually/instead* trong script; chép 4 câu dictation.
3. Nghe lại sau 48 giờ.

## Unit 2 — Section 3: Matching (project roles)

**Mục tiêu:** Ghép người với vai trò khi có trao đổi, từ chối và đổi ý.  
**Script:** 357 từ · 3 người nói · 130–140 từ/phút.

### Vocabulary

| Word/phrase | IPA | Nghĩa |
|---|---|---|
| audit | /ˈɔːdɪt/ | đợt kiểm tra, đánh giá |
| meter reading | /ˈmiːtə ˌriːdɪŋ/ | chỉ số đồng hồ |
| proofread | /ˈpruːfriːd/ | đọc soát lỗi |
| referencing | /ˈrefrənsɪŋ/ | trích dẫn tài liệu |

### External sources

- **BBC — English at University:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/english-at-university — nhận và từ chối nhiệm vụ.
- **British Council — B2 Listening:** https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/listening/b2-listening — bài matching.
- **ESL Lab:** https://www.esl-lab.com/ — mục *School / University*.

### Script

> **Dr Okafor:** Right, the campus sustainability audit. We've got five weeks. Have you two talked about who does what?
>
> **Priya:** A bit. I'd assumed I'd do the data analysis, because I'm doing statistics this term. But the meter readings have to be collected on Wednesdays, and I have a lab then.
>
> **Ben:** I could take the readings. I'm on campus on Wednesdays anyway. Though — actually, I'd rather do the analysis. I'm comfortable with spreadsheets, and I was going to suggest the presentation, but standing up and talking isn't really me.
>
> **Dr Okafor:** Let's be practical. Priya, you've done interviews before, haven't you? The café staff and the cleaning team are the people who see waste up close. Someone needs to talk to them.
>
> **Priya:** I have, yes. I did twelve interviews for the library project. I'd be happy to do that.
>
> **Dr Okafor:** Good. And I'd rather have one person do all the interviews, so the questions stay the same.
>
> **Priya:** That makes sense. How many should I aim for?
>
> **Dr Okafor:** Eight to ten. Recorded, if they agree. And delete the recordings once they're transcribed. Ben, the analysis is yours, then. Not just averages — compare the readings by building and by day of the week.
>
> **Ben:** Yes. I'll need the estates office data, though. They have the electricity figures for the whole campus.
>
> **Dr Okafor:** I'll email them today. Priya, draft the interview questions this week; send them to me first.
>
> **Priya:** Will do.
>
> **Dr Okafor:** Now, the presentation: that should be collaborative. All three of us, each taking one section.
>
> **Priya:** Fine. And the report?
>
> **Dr Okafor:** I'll do the final proofread and check the referencing — that's my job, not yours. But you write the drafts. First draft by Thursday week.
>
> **Ben:** Thursday the... sorry, Thursday week meaning the one after next?
>
> **Dr Okafor:** The one after next. The budget's handled by the department, and we're not doing social media — this is an internal audit. Keep the interviews anonymous; no names in the report or the transcript. Oh, and could one of you book the presentation room? It fills up quickly.

1. What is the purpose of the meeting?
2. How long is the project?
3. What is Dr Okafor's attitude to the presentation?

A. data analysis B. interviews with staff C. presentation D. final proofread and referencing E. booking the presentation room F. budget management G. social media

**4.** Priya — _____  
**5.** Ben — _____  
**6.** Dr Okafor — _____  
**7.** Which task has not been assigned to anyone? — _____  
**8.** Why does Ben change his mind about the presentation?  
A. He dislikes public speaking. B. He has a Wednesday lab. C. He wants to work with Priya.

**Questions 9–12: Dictation**

9. The meter readings have to be collected on Wednesdays.
10. I'm comfortable with spreadsheets.
11. I'll do the final proofread and check the referencing.
12. Could one of you book the presentation room?

### Answer key

1. To allocate roles for the sustainability audit.
2. Five weeks.
3. It should be shared: each takes one section.
4. **B**
5. **A**
6. **D**
7. **E** — F and G are explicitly excluded.
8. **A** — "standing up and talking isn't really me".
9. The meter readings have to be collected on Wednesdays.
10. I'm comfortable with spreadsheets.
11. I'll do the final proofread and check the referencing.
12. Could one of you book the presentation room?

### Pronunciation & strategy

- **audit** /ˈɔːdɪt/ — nhấn âm 1; không thêm /n/.
- **analysis** /əˈnæləsɪs/ — nhấn âm 2; số nhiều /-siːz/.

Đáp án matching thường **không được nhắc nguyên văn**. Priya nói "I'd assumed I'd do the data analysis" → distractor; đáp án đến sau *but*. Ben đổi từ presentation sang analysis. Budget và social media được nhắc để **loại**.

### Study method

1. Làm bài 2 lần; lần 2 chỉ nghe matching.
2. Viết một câu giải thích vì sao loại F và G.
3. Đọc script theo ba vai; nghe lại sau 2 ngày.

## Unit 3 — Section 4: Lecture structure & signposting

**Mục tiêu:** Theo dõi bố cục bài giảng nhờ cụm dẫn dắt; ghi đúng nguyên nhân, đo lường, giải pháp.  
**Script:** 365 từ · 1 người nói · 140–150 từ/phút.

### Vocabulary

| Word/phrase | IPA | Nghĩa |
|---|---|---|
| urban heat island | /ˌɜːbən ˈhiːt ˌaɪlənd/ | đảo nhiệt đô thị |
| tarmac | /ˈtɑːmæk/ | nhựa đường |
| transpiration | /ˌtrænspəˈreɪʃn/ | sự thoát hơi nước |
| intervention | /ˌɪntəˈvenʃn/ | biện pháp can thiệp |

### External sources

- **BBC Radio 4 — In Our Time:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b006qykl — một tập về *cities, climate*.
- **Gresham College:** https://www.gresham.ac.uk/ — public lecture có transcript.
- **TED-Ed:** https://ed.ted.com/ — video ngắn về *urban planning, heat*.

### Script

> Good morning. Today I want to look at a phenomenon most of you have experienced without naming: the urban heat island. Put simply, a city is often several degrees warmer than the countryside around it — in large cities, up to seven degrees on a still, clear night. I'll begin with the causes, then say a word about how researchers measure the effect, and finish with two responses and one caution.
>
> First, the causes. There are three, and they interact. The most obvious is the replacement of vegetation with hard, dark surfaces. Tarmac and dark roofing absorb sunlight during the day and release it slowly at night, so the city never cools properly. Second, cities generate heat of their own: vehicles, factories and, increasingly, air-conditioning units, which cool buildings by pushing warmth into the street. Third — and this is the one people forget — tall buildings trap that heat near the ground, reducing the wind that would otherwise carry it away.
>
> Now, measurement. You might assume the difference is simply read from weather stations, but official stations are usually sited in parks or on open ground, precisely to avoid local effects. Researchers therefore use mobile sensors, often mounted on bicycles or trams, to build a picture street by street. The results can be surprising: the hottest point is not always the city centre but a large car park or a supermarket roof.
>
> That brings me to responses. The first is planting trees. Trees cool the air in two ways: through shade and through transpiration — water evaporating from leaves. A mature street tree can be worth several air-conditioning units. The second is cool roofs: pale surfaces that reflect sunlight rather than absorb it. Both work, but neither is universal. Trees need water and space, and in some streets they trap pollution at the level where people breathe. Cool roofs help most in hot, sunny climates and do little in winter.
>
> To sum up: heat islands are produced by surfaces, waste heat and geometry together. No single measure will fix them. The most successful schemes combine several small interventions — a row of trees here, a pale roof there — rather than one large one.

### Tasks

**Questions 1–3: Gist**

1. What is the lecture mainly about?
2. How many causes are described?
3. What is the lecturer's view of single solutions?

**Questions 4–8: Note completion** — **NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER**.

| Urban heat islands | |
|---|---|
| Temperature | Up to **4** ______ warmer on a still, clear night |
| Causes | Tall buildings reduce the **5** ______ near the ground |
| Measurement | Mobile sensors are mounted on bicycles or **6** ______ |
| Trees | Cool the air through shade and **7** ______ |
| Caution | In some streets they trap **8** ______ where people breathe |

**Questions 9–12: Dictation**

9. A city is often several degrees warmer than the countryside around it.
10. Tarmac and dark roofing absorb sunlight during the day.
11. Official stations are usually sited in parks or on open ground.
12. No single measure will fix the problem.

### Answer key

1. The causes, measurement and responses to urban heat islands.
2. Three.
3. They are insufficient; several small interventions are needed.
4. seven degrees
5. wind
6. trams
7. transpiration
8. pollution
9. A city is often several degrees warmer than the countryside around it.
10. Tarmac and dark roofing absorb sunlight during the day.
11. Official stations are usually sited in parks or on open ground.
12. No single measure will fix the problem.

### Pronunciation & strategy

- **island** /ˈaɪlənd/ — /s/ câm; *heat island* nối /t‿aɪ/.
- **absorb** /əbˈzɔːb/ — nhấn âm 2; không có /p/.

Signposting là bản đồ Section 4: *Today I want to look at...*, *I'll begin with..., then..., and finish with...*, *First / Second / Third*, *That brings me to...*, *To sum up*. Kẻ 3–4 ô từ câu giới thiệu. Bẫy: "You might assume..." mở đầu một ý **sai**, sửa sau *but*.

### Study method

1. Nghe một lần, ghi từ khóa theo dàn bài 3 phần.
2. Nghe lần hai, điền 4–8; kiểm tra số ít/số nhiều.
3. Tô màu signposting; tự giảng lại 3 phút.

## Unit 4 — Note-taking dưới áp lực tốc độ

**Mục tiêu:** Ghi chú khi bài giảng tăng tốc; bắt số liệu mà không cần câu hoàn chỉnh.  
**Script:** 375 từ · 1 người nói · 150–165 từ/phút.

### Vocabulary

| Word/phrase | IPA | Nghĩa |
|---|---|---|
| food miles | /ˈfuːd maɪlz/ | quãng đường vận chuyển thực phẩm |
| emission | /ɪˈmɪʃn/ | khí thải |
| shrinkage | /ˈʃrɪŋkɪdʒ/ | hao hụt hàng hóa |
| cold chain | /ˈkəʊld tʃeɪn/ | chuỗi bảo quản lạnh |

### External sources

- **BBC — 6 Minute English:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/6-minute-english — *food waste, food miles*.
- **TED-Ed:** https://ed.ted.com/ — video 4–6 phút về *food systems*.
- **ELLLO:** https://www.elllo.org/ — level 6–7 để luyện tốc độ.

### Script

> Right, I'm going to move quickly today, so please write in note form rather than full sentences. And please don't try to write down every number I say; the sheet has them. The topic is the hidden journey of food — and I want to challenge two assumptions.
>
> The first assumption is that food miles are the best measure of a food's environmental cost. It sounds obvious: the further something travels, the worse it is. But transport is usually a small part of the total. For most foods, growing, processing and, above all, waste matter far more. A tomato flown from Kenya in winter may produce fewer emissions than one grown in a heated greenhouse in northern Europe. The reason is energy: heating a glasshouse for months uses more fuel than a single flight. None of this means transport is irrelevant; aviation emissions are real. But we should not confuse the most visible part of a system with the most important one.
>
> The second assumption is that waste is a consumer problem — that if shoppers planned better, the problem would disappear. In fact, in higher-income countries, roughly a third of food is wasted, and a large share of that happens before it reaches the shop: crops rejected for shape, orders cancelled, cold stores failing. ### Scripts

#### Section 1

>
> **Caller:** Hello, I'm calling about the bicycle maintenance course — the one on Saturdays.

> **Receptionist:** Ah yes. The next course starts on 12 April. It was going to be the 6th, but that's Easter weekend, so we moved it.

>
> **Caller:** And the cost?
>

>
> **Caller:** I'm not a student. Do I need to bring my own bike?

#### Section 2

> Good afternoon, and welcome to the City Library. My name's Alan Price, and I look after the local history room. I'm here to tell you about some changes.
>
> The biggest is that the room has moved. For years it was in the basement — dark, and frankly damp. Since January it's been on the second floor, next to the reading room, with proper daylight and twice the shelf space. The collection itself hasn't changed.
>
> Who can use it? Anyone with a library card. You don't need to be a student or a member of the history society. Bring your card, and a pencil — pens aren't allowed near the documents.
>
> Most of the room is free to use. Photocopying costs ten pence a sheet; scanning is free. Research help is free for the first hour, then twenty pounds an hour — but most people don't need it.
>
> We're looking for volunteers, but not for shelving. We need people to help catalogue the photograph collection — about four thousand images, many unidentified. If you recognise a street or a building, that's exactly the skill we need. We're not asking for old books or newspapers; we have more than we can store.
>
> And what do people actually come for? Not the maps, surprisingly, and not the newspapers. It's the photographs — family groups, shopfronts, the harbour before the redevelopment.

#### Section 3

> **Dr Nunes:** Right, the field trip report. We need five sections, and I want to agree who writes what now.
>
> **Jess:** I assumed I'd do the introduction.
>
> **Dr Nunes:** Good — but the introduction is aims and context, not a literature review. Two pages at most.
>
> **Marco:** I thought the introduction should review all the studies.
>
> **Dr Nunes:** No. Keep it short. Marco, you'll write the methods — exactly what we did, in the order we did it, so that someone could repeat the work.
>
> **Marco:** Including the equipment list?
>
> **Dr Nunes:** The equipment list goes in the appendix. The methods section describes the procedure, not the shopping list.
>
> **Jess:** And the results?
>
> **Dr Nunes:** The results are the data — measurements, tables, no interpretation. Interpretation belongs in the discussion.
>
> **Marco:** So who writes the results?
>
> **Dr Nunes:** You both do. Jess, you'll present the chemical data; Marco, the invertebrate counts. I'll write the discussion, because it has to link back to the aims. And the appendix — raw readings, the questionnaire, the equipment list — we'll compile together.

#### Section 4

> Good morning. Today's lecture is about paper — a material so ordinary that we rarely ask where it came from. I want to trace three moments: its invention, its arrival in Europe, and its transformation into a cheap industrial product.
>
> The story begins in China. For centuries people wrote on bamboo, silk or wood. Then, around 105 CE, a court official named Cai Lun is credited with a method for making paper from plant fibres, waste cloth and bark. The recipe spread slowly — first through Korea and Japan, then westwards along trade routes. It reached the Islamic world in the eighth century, and from there, through Spain and Sicily, into Europe. So the second moment is not a European invention at all; Europe learned the technique from the Islamic world.
>
> For centuries, paper was made from rags. That limited supply and kept prices high. Then, in the nineteenth century, manufacturers began to use wood pulp. The change was dramatic: paper became cheap, and cheap paper made popular newspapers possible. It also, unfortunately, made paper fragile — the acid in wood pulp turns yellow and brittle within decades, which is why so many nineteenth-century books are now crumbling.
>
> One more point, because it matters today. Making paper from wood pulp uses a great deal of water and energy. Recycled paper typically uses about a third less energy than virgin paper, though the fibre shortens each time it is recycled. So recycling is not infinite.
>
> To conclude: paper was invented in China, arrived in Europe through the Islamic world, and was democratised by wood pulp. Each step changed who could read — and what they could afford to read.

## Tổng kết lộ trình

1. **Nghe được ý, không chỉ từ:** kể lại một lecture 3 phút bằng 5 câu tiếng Anh, không nhìn script.
2. **Phát hiện distractor ngay:** đánh dấu `!` mỗi khi nghe *but, actually, instead, sorry*.
3. **Giữ bình tĩnh khi mất câu:** bỏ tối đa 1 câu mỗi section, không cần nghe hết 100%.

**Lịch 4 tuần:** 2 unit mới + 1 bài cũ mỗi tuần; 15 phút/ngày; 5 câu dictation/ngày; cuối tuần làm lại mock.

**Nguồn ngoài:** link là cấp trang/chuyên mục, không phải link sâu. Nếu link đổi, vào trang chủ và tìm theo từ khóa trong mô tả. Không tải hoặc đăng lại audio, transcript của nguồn khác.
